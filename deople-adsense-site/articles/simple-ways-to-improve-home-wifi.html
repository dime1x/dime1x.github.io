Simple Ways to Improve Your Home Wi-Fi Connection

A slow connection does not always mean you need a new internet plan or router. The cause may be router placement, interference, an outdated device, or one application using a large share of your bandwidth.

Published 2026-11-08 · 7 min read · Deople Editorial Team

Place the router in a useful location

Put the router in an open, central position when possible. Thick walls, metal objects, and enclosed cabinets can weaken signals. If the router must sit near one edge of the home, consider whether a mesh system or access point would provide better coverage.

Restart and update equipment safely

A restart can clear temporary problems, but it is not a permanent fix for every issue. Check the router manufacturer’s instructions for firmware updates and install them through the official administration interface. Avoid firmware files from unofficial websites.

Understand the Wi-Fi bands

Many routers offer 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz networks. The 2.4 GHz band often reaches farther and passes through obstacles more effectively, while 5 GHz can offer higher speeds at shorter distances. Newer equipment may also support 6 GHz, subject to device and regional compatibility.

Test more than one device

Run a speed test near the router and then in the room where the connection is weak. Compare a wired connection, if available, with Wi-Fi. If only one device is slow, check its software, background downloads, and network settings before changing the whole network.

Reduce avoidable congestion

Pause large downloads or cloud backups while testing. Disconnect devices that are no longer in use and check the router’s connected-device list for anything unfamiliar. Change the Wi-Fi password if you suspect unauthorized access.

Know when to contact your provider

If a wired test is consistently far below the plan’s expected performance, record the results and contact your internet provider. Ask whether there is an outage or line issue. Keep in mind that advertised speeds, actual speeds, and Wi-Fi performance are different measurements.

Key takeaway

Check placement, compare multiple devices, test near the router, and separate Wi-Fi problems from internet-service problems before buying new equipment.
